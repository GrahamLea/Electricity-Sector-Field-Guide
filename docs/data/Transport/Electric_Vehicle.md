# Electric Vehicle
#UsedVeryFrequently

## Synonyms
- Electrified Transport
- Electric Car

## Abbreviations
- EV


## Summary

Any vehicle propelled by electric power.
Most commonly used to refer to road-going cars, motorcycles, trucks, and buses, that use
a [battery] as a major source of energy for moving.


## More

Technically this term also includes any vehicle (a machine for moving people or goods) 
with an [electric motor] as part of its propulsion, 
for instance bicycles, scooters, trains, boats, and aeroplanes.
The term "e-mobility" is sometimes used to refer to this wider range of electric transport,
in an effort to disambiguate from electric cars.
(The rest of this entry is specific to electric road vehicles.)

Some electric vehicles may only have electric motors, 
while others combine an electric motor with a fuel-based engine.
While the electric motor requires a source of electric charge for [power], 
typically a battery,
the [energy] for that power may have been sourced externally to the vehicle, 
but could also be sourced exclusively from the combustion of fuel in the same vehicle, 
possibly via kinetic energy and [regenerative braking].
All this leads to the sub-categories of [battery electric vehicle]s (or "full EVs"),
[hybrid electric vehicle]s, and [plug-in hybrid electric vehicle]s.

Non-plug-in hybrids are often excluded from "electric vehicle" statistics, 
due to the electric motors being a fuel-saving supplementary component,
with all the vehicle's energy still being introduced by fuels (typically combusted fossil fuels).

In a residential setting, EV charging will typically be the single highest-power activity, 
typically using 7-25kW if [hard-wired] [EVSE] is installed.
It may not be the largest energy usage, as appliances like water heaters and [HVAC]
may run at lower power but for much longer times.

Electrified transport is an important part of the global transition to a low-GHG emissions economy.
It has long been the second-highest contributing sector to global emissions, 
after "electricity and heat", accounting for ~17% of CO2e emissions in 2023.
While aviation and shipping often receive attention for their emissions,
in 2018 the majority of transport emissions were from road transport, 
primarily passenger vehicles (45%) and freight (29%).
Transport is now becoming the largest source of emissions 
in some countries that have made a lot of progress in deploying [renewable] electricity.
