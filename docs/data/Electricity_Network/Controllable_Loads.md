# Controllable Loads

## Summary

Energy-consuming devices that can change their real-time load on a [grid] to achieve some aim,
usually by reducing their power, but also potentially by increasing it.


## More

Devices may be controlled for the purpose of bolstering [grid] security with [demand management],
or to achieve economic goals, for example by running high-power loads during the cheap periods of
a [time-of-use tariff].
They may be controlled by the owner, for example with a [Home Energy Management System], 
or by a third-party, such as occurs with [dynamic exports].

Examples of controllable loads include hot water storage systems, EV charging, [HVAC], batteries, 
and also large industrial loads, such as electric smelters and utility water pumps.

In Australian energy markets, the term "[controlled load]" has a specific meaning used in reference
to house circuits, metering, and tariffs.
